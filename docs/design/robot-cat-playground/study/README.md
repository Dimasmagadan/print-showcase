# Playground composition and raster study

`index.html` is a static desktop/mobile composition study, not the finished interactive. The cat and duck controls illustrate the two assembly modes and the exploded joints. The study uses a common orthographic camera and locally exported model geometry; source meshes stay outside the site.

The initial layer experiment is **not approved for production compositing**: stacking isolated body, leg and head renders shows the ends of the body's leg pins over the assembled legs and removes contact shadows. A production transition needs depth-aware masks or pre-rendered transition frames, with a check at intermediate poses. The cart wheels also need occlusion masks and a distance-linked rotation study. The belly image requires a perspective mapping and clipping check. The storyboard images are not evidence for those behaviors.

The later full raster set uses a wider camera to keep the rabbit ears inside the frame when exploded. `scripts/robot-cat-playground-render.py` generates its 12 assembled and 12 exploded views plus isolated layers in `/tmp`; `scripts/robot-cat-playground-media.mjs` compresses them. The generated set, including intermediate poses and wheel phases, currently measures about 2.30 MiB; this is not a decoded-memory or network-concurrency measurement. Full-scene intermediate poses now avoid the observed layer overlap problem. Mode and body changes now use separate full-scene support poses; the body, arms and tail move together, and robot support leaves before the cart enters. The controller now uses explicit 640×640 ImageBitmaps with an 18-frame cap; cancelled and completed transitions close their temporary bitmaps. The simulated worst case reaches 29,491,200 decoded bitmap bytes. It waits for the story frame pool to drain before loading; on reverse scroll it releases its retained frames while keeping the painted canvas, then restores the selected assembly when the section is approached again. This is instrumented controller accounting, not a browser-wide memory measurement. Browser visual and device gesture checks are still outstanding.

The wheel study uses a subtle procedural surface treatment to help make rotation visible; the same render material is used in the cart frames. These are renders and do not assert the finish of a physically printed cart.

The `review/` contact sheets cover all twelve assemblies, their exploded states, and the cat robot-to-cart sequence. A contact-sheet audit caught a positional reset error in three cart posters; the corrected exports are checked for clipping and travel boundaries by the asset tests. Browser screenshots remain outstanding.
